B

Bottomland Forest #

Bottomland hardwood forests are river swamps. They are found along rivers and streams in the southeastern and south-central United States, generally in broad floodplains. These ecosystems are commonly found wherever streams or rivers at least occasionally cause flooding beyond their channel confines (EPA).

C

Clean Water Act #

Establishes the basic structure for regulating discharges of pollutants into the waters of the United States and regulating quality standards for surface waters. The basis of the CWA was enacted in 1948 and was called the Federal Water Pollution Control Act, but the Act was significantly reorganized and expanded in 1972. "Clean Water Act" became the Act's common name with amendments in 1972 (EPA).

Containment Barrier #

Refers to structures, physical barriers, or completely enclosed units designed under U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) regulations to stop hazardous wastes, chemicals, oils, or lead dust from escaping into the environment, groundwater, or surrounding areas (EPA).

E

Effluent #

Treated or untreated liquid waste, including sewage, industrial chemicals, or water from manufacturing or commercial processes that leaves a facility, such as a wastewater treatment plant, and enters a natural water body (EPA).

Endocrine-Disrupting Compounds #

Natural or man-made substances that mimic, block, or interfere with the body's natural hormones and disrupt normal endocrine system functions (Endocrine Society).

H

Heavy Metals (Cadmium, Chromium, Copper, Lead, Zinc) #

Naturally occurring. Also used in various industries, including textiles, electroplating, and metal finishing. These stable elements do not break down in the environment, remain bound to riverbed sediments indefinitely, and can affect multiple organ systems, including known or suspected carcinogens (EPA).

Hydrologic Unit Codes #

Watershed sizes are classified using Hydrologic Unit Codes (HUCs), a standard system for identifying and delineating watersheds from the smallest to the largest. These areas do not exist in isolation; smaller watersheds nest within the bounds of larger watersheds (USGS).

I

Impervious Surface #

A hard surface area that either prevents or retards the entry of water into the soil mantle or causes water to run off the surface in greater quantities or at an increased rate of flow. Common impervious surfaces include rooftops, walkways, patios, driveways, parking lots, storage areas, concrete or asphalt paving, and compacted gravel roads (EPA).

P

Penstock Gate #

A type of sluice gate or flow control device typically installed in open channels or pipelines to regulate the flow of water or other fluids. It can be raised, lowered, or sealed off completely to control, direct, or stop the movement of water (Enke Group).

Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances (PFAS) #

Commonly known as ‘forever chemicals,’ these artificial chemicals are found in non-stick cookware, waterproof and fire-resistant clothing, stain-resistant carpets, food packaging, and firefighting foam. PFAS are extremely persistent in the environment (hence the ‘forever’ distinction). PFAS are known to accumulate in blood, soil, water, and wildlife and are associated with human health risks, including reproductive and developmental effects, cancer risk, compromised immune system, and more (EPA).

Pervious Surface #

A porous surface area that allows rainwater to soak naturally into the ground, reducing stormwater runoff and filtering out pollutants. Common pervious surfaces include grassy areas, gardens, permeable pavers, unpaved walkways, and mulch.

Pesticides #

Substances intended to kill or control pests such as weeds, insects, rodents, fungi, bacteria, or other organisms. The human health effects vary by pesticide type; however, they may affect the nervous system, be carcinogens, or affect the body's hormone or endocrine system (EPA).

Polychlorinated Biphenyls (PCBs) #

Used in electrical transformers and capacitors, in some textile processing, and in a variety of other industrial processes. These chemicals have been demonstrated to be linked to a variety of adverse human (and animal) health effects, including reproductive effects, developmental effects, neurological effects, endocrine disruption, cancer risks, compromised immune system, and more (EPA).

Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons (PAHs) #

Found in coal tar, coal gas, creosote, road and roofing tar, asphalt, and petroleum distillates. These chemicals can impact human health in a variety of ways depending on the type of PAHs, and may cause “harmful effects on skin, body fluids, and the body’s system for fighting disease.” Several of these chemicals are known carcinogens (CDC).

Prograding Delta #

A deposit of sediment that progressively transforms a water body into a terrestrial environment.

R

Restrictive Covenant #

A legally binding clause in a contract or property deed that limits, prohibits, or regulates specific actions by a person, business, or property owner (Investopedia).

S

Sedimentation #

When water slows down, such as when it flows from a creek into a lake, suspended sediment can drop out of suspension and settle on the lake bed. This is known as sedimentation, and when sedimentation rates are high and the sediment collects in a lake bed, this can lead to lake infilling, where the volume of the lake shrinks as it is filled with sediment (USGS).

South Carolina’s Erosion and Sediment Reduction Act (Title 48, Chapter 14 and Chapter 18 of the S.C. Code of Laws) #

An Act that regulates land-disturbing activities to control soil erosion, sedimentation, and stormwater runoff (scstatehouse.gov).

South Carolina’s Pollution Control Act (Title 48, Chapter 1 of the South Carolina Code of Laws) #

The foundational state law governing air and water quality protection (scstatehouse.gov).

South Carolina’s Stormwater Management and Sediment Reduction Act #

An Act that (Title 48, Chapter 14 of the South Carolina Code of Laws) regulates land-disturbing activities to control erosion, reduce sediment, and manage stormwater runoff (scstatehouse.gov).

Stormwater Detention Pond #

Wet ponds (also referred to as stormwater ponds, wet retention ponds, or wet extended detention ponds) are constructed basins that have a permanent pool of water throughout the year (or at least throughout the wet season) (EPA).

Stormwater Runoff #

Stormwater runoff is rain and snowmelt that flows over the ground rather than infiltrating it, often picking up contaminants such as motor oil, heavy metals, and fertilizers and discharging them untreated into local waterways.

Superfund Site #

Any land in the United States that has been severely contaminated by hazardous waste and designated by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency for long-term cleanup (EPA).

V

Voluntary Cleanup Contract #

In South Carolina, this is a legally binding agreement between the South Carolina Department of Environmental Services (SCDES, formerly DHEC) and a property owner or prospective buyer to assess, clean up, and redevelop contaminated or underutilized industrial sites (Brownfields) (DES).

W

Watershed #

A watershed is a specific area of land where all rainfall, snowmelt, and surface runoff drains downhill into a common outlet, such as a stream, river, lake, or ocean (NOAA).

Wetlands #

Wetlands are areas where water covers the soil, or is present at or near the soil surface year-round or for varying periods during the year, including the growing season (EPA).
