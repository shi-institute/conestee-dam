Containment: The Conestee Dam Story

Since 1892, a dam on the Reedy River has shaped the land, the water, and the communities around it. Explore how Lake Conestee came to hold more than a century of sediment, and how a new dam now keeps it in place.

A river's history, settled in sediment

Built in 1892 to power a textile mill, the Lake Conestee Dam has stood for more than a century, and its reservoir has shaped the area through many phases of its history. Over those decades, mills, power plants, sewage, and industry sent contaminated sediment down the Reedy River, and much of it came to rest behind the dam.

Today, the new Lake Conestee Dam acts as a containment barrier so that sediment cannot be released if the old dam were to fail. The lake is also home to a nature preserve. These stories walk through how we arrived here.
