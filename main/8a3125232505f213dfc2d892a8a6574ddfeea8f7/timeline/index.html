Timeline

Early land ownership in the future Lake Conestee area

1785

Land records show early ownership of property in the area that would become part of the Lake Conestee site. The development of multiple dams and mills is known to have occurred as early as the 1790s.

The first land grant along the Reedy River is made, followed by another in 1794, and the development of multiple dams and mills.

The first dam and mill are built at Lafferty’s Shoal

1793

Initial dam and mill at Lafferty’s Shoal, owned by John Goodwyn, Gilbert Sweat, and later Carruth & Alston.

Carruth’s Armory builds a dam and begins manufacturing muskets

1815

A dam associated with Carruth’s Armory is documented and known to be near the upper end of what would be the future Lake Conestee. Carruth’s armory, which had now constructed a musket factory and accompanying dam, begins manufacturing muskets for the cause of the War of 1812.

A village develops around the mills

1820

A village is developed around the mills at the Reedy River.

Carruth’s Armory fails and its property is sold

1824

Carruth’s Armory failed, and the 900-acre property was seized by the Greenville Sheriff. The property was then sold to Robert Anderson.

Fire destroys the Carruth’s Armory dam

1826

The dam associated with Carruth’s Armory was completely destroyed by a fire (leaving no dam at that site until 1834).

Vardry McBee builds the earliest documented dam at the Conestee Mill site

1831

Vardry McBee constructed the earliest dam documented at the future site of Conestee Mill. This dam powered his textile operations (Reedy River Factory). That dam was likely a timber crib-and-plank structure. A grist and sawmill were operating just upstream as early as the 1790s, but site conditions suggest a dam did not power it.

A new dam and paper mill are built at the former Carruth site

1834–1842

Andrew Patterson erected a new dam at the former Carruth site (1834) and operated a paper mill (1836). Benajah Dunham acquired it in 1840, elevated the dam, and took over operations following a legal dispute in 1842.

Vardry McBee sells the Reedy River Factory

1862

Vardry McBee sold his Reedy River Factory property to Grady, Hawthorn, & Perry for $21,000, placing the mill under CSA Quartermaster control during the Civil War.

Vardry McBee dies

1863

Death of Vardry McBee.

The Reedy River Manufacturing Company is chartered

1875 & 1877

The Reedy River Manufacturing Company (RRMC) received its first charter in 1875 with Joseph A. David as president. Joel D. Charles took over management in 1877 when David fell ill.

Federal water power census confirms wooden dams on the Reedy River

1885

A Federal census of water power in the region confirmed a wooden dam in existence at the Reedy River Manufacturing Company (RRMC) site. Another wooden dam was still present at the location of the old Carruth’s Armory, one mile upstream.

Land is acquired for a new dam and reservoir

1890

Lidie Morgan purchased the 50-acre Gun Factory / Dunham paper mill tract and sold it to RRMC specifically to construct a new dam and clear area for reservoir flooding.

Major textile mills are built along the Reedy River

1890-1915

Construction of ~12 major textile mills along the Reedy River. In 1930, the mill villages had an estimated population of 29,000 residents.

Greenville constructs a sewer system that discharges into the Reedy River

1892

A sewer system is constructed for the City of Greenville; the collection system, which did not have an accompanying treatment facility, discharges at the junction of Richland Creek and Reedy River (present-day Cleveland Park).

The stone-masonry Lake Conestee Dam is completed

1892

The stone-masonry dam built on the Reedy River here, by Joel D. Charles, is completed. It provided mechanical power to his growing Reedy River Manufacturing Company’s textile factory. This structure was a remarkable feat of engineering and construction for its time. It created a large reservoir of roughly 130 acres.

The Reedy River Manufacturing Company built a dam across the Reedy River to provide mechanical power for its growing textile factory. This dam and power works were first converted to hydroelectric power in 1916, providing more power for what was then Conestee Mill. The 1892 stone-masonry dam was a remarkable construction achievement when it was built. It created a large reservoir, extraordinary for its time at 140 acres.

(This dam remains today, though it is now submerged by the New Lake Conestee Dam, which was completed in 2025 and is located immediately downstream of the old dam. This new structure provides a ‘containment barrier’ to keep the massive amount of contaminated sediment behind it from being released if the old dam were to fail.)

Flood ‘of Biblical proportion’

1908

The record flood of 1908 occurs in this region, described as ‘of Biblical proportion.’ The stone-masonry dam survives but has incurred limited damage. Two of the factory’s three operational floors were severely flooded. This flood destroyed many bridges, roads, and other structures.

Conestee Mills is chartered

1909

RRMC went through receivership and was sold at a referee’s sale to W.E. Beattie & Associates. Conestee Mills was officially chartered in July 1909, with 28-year-old T.I. Charles as president.

Typhoid fever breaks out in Conestee and Greenville

1915

Typhoid fever broke out in Conestee and the greater Greenville area. Employees at Conestee Mills abandoned their houses along the Reedy River and, in 1916, refused to move into new houses near the reservoir and along the river.

Cattle die from drinking Reedy River water

1917

Cattle in pastures died from drinking water from the Reedy River.

The Conestee Village company store is built

1919

Construction of the Conestee Village company store.

City determines need for a sewage disposal plant

1923

The City determined a need for a sewage disposal plant for Greenville. Reedy River and Lake Conestee conditions “at their worst.”

Conestee Mills sues the City of Greenville due to raw sewage

1925

Conestee Mills sued the City of Greenville for financial damages due to raw sewage contaminating Lake Conestee and Reedy River: “The water has become so foul and contaminated as to be a source of disease and constant peril to the health and lives of the operatives of Conestee Mills.” (Conestee Mills v. City of Greenville, amended complaint 1929).

Greater Greenville Sanitation District is created

1926

The Greater Greenville Sanitation District is created (now ReWa), tasked with overseeing the construction of Greenville’s first ‘modern’ wastewater treatment plant.

Greenville’s first wastewater treatment plant is constructed

1928

The first wastewater treatment plant to serve the City of Greenville was constructed at the present Mauldin Road plant facility site. The Greater Greenville Sanitary Commission built the plant, which was subsequently WCRSA and is now ReWa.

Mills Mill discharges sewage into a nearby stream

1928

Mills Mill (Brushy Creek) discharged sewage into a nearby stream.

Conestee Mills president testifies about gas plant pollution

1930

President of Conestee Mills testified, “Gas plant emptying sulfur, acids, and poisons that killed fish (in Lake Conestee).”

Conestee Mills v. City of Greenville is settled

1931

After two rounds of hearings by the SC Supreme Court, the parties reached a settlement. Six years of litigation in Conestee Mills v. City of Greenville resulted in the City of Greenville paying $75,000 in financial damages.

Mills close, but legacy pollution remains

1931-1999

During the Great Depression, Conestee Mills closed in 1939 and never operated again as a locally owned textile plant. Over the next several decades, other mills are sold and cease operations, marking the sunset of Greenville’s days as the “Textile Center of the World.”

Remedial activities to address legacy pollution at many sites are ongoing. For example, substantial cleanup at Union Bleachery has occurred, and the Broad Street Manufactured Gas Plant has been thoroughly remediated. Still other sites, such as the Bramlett Manufactured Gas Plant, have undergone substantial cleanup efforts, but an additional major remediation project is planned there.

Some sites, such as the Greenville Steam Plant, a coal-fired power plant operating from 1909 to the 1950s, have received little attention, although they certainly had a significant impact. Although much has been learned about industrial contamination in this watershed since the 1970s, new sites and sources of contamination will continue to be recognized.

Greenville Army Air Base is constructed

1943

The construction of the Greenville Army Air Base (Donaldson Air Force Base) occurs. Sediments associated with development and runoff from the northern portion of the installation may have contributed to sediment loading in Marrow Bone Creek, a tributary of the Reedy River.

First municipal landfill operates along the Reedy River

1950s-1960s

First municipal landfill is constructed and in operation along the banks of the Reedy River. The Greenville Technical College campus is now located at this site.

Interstate 85 is constructed through Greenville

1959-1964

Interstate 85 is constructed through the Greenville area, adding substantial infrastructure and associated land disturbance upstream of Lake Conestee.

Second municipal landfill operates along the Reedy River

1960s-1970s

Second municipal landfill is constructed and is in operation along the banks of the Reedy River. This landfill was closed in the early 1990s.

Interstate 385 is constructed in phases

1963-1984

Interstate 385 is constructed in phases, bringing additional road development and associated stormwater runoff and sediment to Lake Conestee.

Federal Clean Water Act is signed into law

1972

The Federal Clean Water Act (CWA) is signed, establishing a strong regulatory authority to protect the nation’s waters, ambitiously aspiring to make them all ‘fishable and swimmable.’

South Carolina’s Erosion and Sediment Reduction Act is enacted

1983

South Carolina’s Erosion and Sediment Reduction Act strengthened requirements to control erosion and sedimentation from land-disturbing activities.

South Carolina’s Pollution Control Act is enacted

1987

South Carolina’s Pollution Control Act provided a broader state framework for environmental pollution control.

South Carolina’s Stormwater Management and Sediment Reduction Act is enacted

1991

South Carolina’s Stormwater Management and Sediment Reduction Act established additional requirements for managing stormwater runoff and sediment.

Conestee Foundation is chartered

1999

Conestee Foundation is chartered as a 501(c)3 public nonprofit organization.

Conestee Foundation acquires the Lake Conestee Dam and Reservoir

2000

Conestee Foundation acquires the Lake Conestee Dam and Reservoir, initiates decades of assessments, studies, design, and permitting, and enters a Voluntary Cleanup Contract (VCC) to establish a long-term management plan for the Lake Conestee Site.

A penstock blockage fails, carving the “Conestee Canyon”

2000–2001

A blockage upstream of the penstock gate spontaneously failed, allowing the entire Reedy River flow to pass through the dam via the 8-ft diameter penstock orifice. The former pool of the Lake quickly disappeared, and the river “Conestee Canyon” flowed through the sediments of the reservoir. Over the ensuing 12 months, more than 90,000 cubic yards of contaminated sediment were released through the dam into the downstream Reedy. In June 2001, temporary gate repairs were completed to halt the erosion and restore containment.

Sampling of the former reservoir reveals widespread contamination

Early 2000s

An extensive program of sampling of sediment, surface water, and groundwater was conducted over the entirety of the former reservoir area. Environmental investigations revealed metals, pesticides, polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs), polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), and other contaminants of concern throughout the Lake’s soils and sediments. Lower concentrations were found in water and groundwater. These investigations established that the sediments and soils were extensively contaminated by a broad array of 20th-century toxins, at concentrations exceeding applicable human health and ecological screening criteria.

SCDHEC determines Lake Conestee is a ‘National Priorities List-Caliber’ site

2004

SCDHEC determined that the Lake Conestee site was a ‘National Priorities List-Caliber’ site. This meant that contamination at the site posed an imminent threat to the public and that it would potentially fully qualify for addition to the Federal Superfund list.

The Voluntary Cleanup Contract is completed and a Restrictive Covenant is established

2006

The VCC was certified as completed, and a Restrictive Covenant was established specifying how the reservoir site must be managed going forward. Elements of this agreement required the Foundation to manage the Lake to keep contaminated sediments in place, limit disturbance of the site, monitor the site’s natural recovery, and prevent human exposure to the contaminants present at the site. The cornerstone of this strategy was to maintain the Lake Conestee Dam and to minimize the migration of contaminated sediments downstream.

The Lake Conestee Dam is up-classified to ‘Significant Risk’

2006

The Lake Conestee Dam was up-classified to a ‘Significant Risk’ status due to revelations of contamination within the structure. This was the first instance of SCDHEC upgrading the risk classification of a dam in SC due to associated contamination issues.

First engineering feasibility study finds the dam at risk of failure

2008

The first engineering feasibility study assessed the condition of the dam, revealing it to be in poor condition, progressively falling apart, unstable, and at risk of catastrophic failure.

Engineering studies examine alternatives for the deteriorating dam

2012 onward

Engineering studies examined the deteriorating dam and alternatives for addressing its structural risks without mobilizing contaminated sediment.

Conestee Mill is added to the National Register of Historic Places

2014

The Conestee Mill / Reedy River Manufacturing site is approved by the US Department of the Interior for addition to the National Register of Historic Places on January 14, 2014.

Dam inspection confirms significant structural concerns

2016

A dam inspection confirmed significant structural concerns, including deterioration and seepage, heightening the need for a long-term solution.

Final feasibility study recommends a new downstream dam

2019

A final, definitive engineering feasibility rigorously reviewed all possible approaches to addressing the old dam and assured long-term containment of contaminated sediments upstream of the 1892 dam. This study identified the Recommended Alternative to be a new concrete dam to be constructed downstream of the old one.

The reservoir shrinks to about 12 acres

2022

The reservoir is reported to have shrunk from approximately 135 acres to about 12 acres, with an estimated 3.25 million cubic yards of sediment accumulated behind the dam.

South Carolina appropriates $39 million for a new dam

2022-2023

The South Carolina General Assembly appropriated $39 million toward the completion of a detailed design for a new dam and for its construction. Additional support from other units of government and others brought the total construction and long-term care budget for the project to roughly $50 million.

Conestee Foundation sells the dam to the Lake Conestee Dam Rehabilitation Project

2023

Conestee Foundation sold the dam and adjacent property to the Lake Conestee Dam Rehabilitation Project (LCDRP).

Property acquisition and site preparation begin

2024

Property acquisition, planning, and site preparation for the new dam began.

Construction of the replacement dam begins

February 2025

Construction of the replacement dam began.

New Lake Conestee Dam is completed

October 2025

A new dam is completed in October 2025, immediately downstream of the old 1892 Lake Conestee Dam. Water began flowing over the newly constructed dam, marking a major milestone in eliminating the risk of failure of the then 133-year-old dam, never intended to provide a waste repository, or to even last for more than 50 years. Thanks to the dedication of the Lake Conestee Dam Rehabilitation Project, the old Lake Conestee Dam no longer presents a risk of physical failure and the catastrophic release of massive quantities of contaminated sediments downstream.

A historical kiosk is constructed on site

2026

A historical kiosk to acknowledge the past is constructed on site to educate visitors, as well as fencing and other protective measures.

Lake Conestee Dam Authority expected to take ownership

2027 (Pending Legislative Action)

Today, the new dam is owned by the Lake Conestee Dam Rehabilitation Project, LLC. Pending legislative authorization, a new governmental entity (the Lake Conestee Dam Authority) is expected to take ownership of the dam in 2027. That entity will be responsible for ongoing maintenance and monitoring of the dam. The agency will be overseen by appointed representatives from Greenville, Laurens, and Greenwood Counties.
